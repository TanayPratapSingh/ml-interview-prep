# Study plan: six weeks, and a two week crash version

This plan takes you through all 12 modules in six weeks, using the cards on the Learn page as the daily core, the guides as reference, and the spoken Drill to practice saying answers aloud. A crash version for two weeks follows it. Both assume about two hours on weekdays and three to four hours on weekend days. If you have less time, drop the practice block first and never skip reviews. For how the rounds themselves work, read [the interview loops guide](#/guide/question-bank/interview-loops), and for what to read and watch, see [the best resources](#/guide/question-bank/resources).

## What you are working with

- **1,501 cards in 12 modules**, 110 to 150 per module, in learning order: each card relies only on earlier cards in its module.
- **The Learn page.** Today's session serves your due reviews first, then the next new cards in module order. The default is 20 new cards a day, and the menu offers 10, 20, 30, 50 and 100. Each module has a tick box, and only ticked modules feed today's session.
- **The spoken Drill.** It gives you a question and a timer (45 seconds by default), you answer aloud, and then it shows the must mention list and the model answer. Each module on the Learn page has a drill aloud link that picks five of its cards. You can also practice any bank question from the Questions page, but bank questions come without a model answer, so check yourself against the matching cards.
- **The Questions page.** 437 real interview questions that you can filter by module, kind, role and level, each linked to where it was seen.
- **Guides.** Each module has a cheatsheet (the ten minutes before a call), a plain guide and a deep guide with matching headings.
- **Lectures.** Four playlists with timestamped notes and their own card decks. The [resources guide](#/guide/question-bank/resources) gives the viewing order.

At 20 new cards a day, six weeks give you 840 new cards, which is 56 percent of 1,501. At 30 a day they give 1,260, which is 84 percent. This plan uses 30, which you set with the New cards a day menu on the Learn page before week 1, and covers the rest on Saturdays. If your reviews take more than about 45 minutes a day, drop back to 20 and let the expert level cards wait.

## The daily rhythm

Planning assumption, not a measurement: a new card takes about a minute and a review about 20 seconds. Thirty new cards plus 50 reviews is then about 45 to 50 minutes.

| Block | Minutes | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today | 10 | Open [Today](#/today). Skim the digest, pick one paper or post, write one question it would raise. Three days a week is enough. |
| Learn | 45 to 50 | Click Start today's session on the [Learn page](#/cards). Say each answer aloud before you flip the card. Rate honestly: a card you guessed is not a card you know. |
| Drill | 15 | Run the module drill: five questions, 45 seconds each, then check the must mention list. Redo any question you missed the next day. |
| Practice | 30 to 45 | The hands on block for that week: SQL, coding, a design or a case. |
| Close | 5 | Write down one thing you could not explain. It becomes tomorrow's first drill question. |

**Weekend.** On Saturday, open the deck of whichever of the week's two modules sits lower on the Learn page list and finish the cards the session did not reach, then do the [Weakest twenty](#/cards/weak) deck and watch the week's lecture. On Sunday, do the session at normal size, one timed mock round, and a 20 minute review of the week's wrong answers.

> **Remember:** A card you rate "easy" and cannot say aloud in 45 seconds is not learned. The spoken answer is the test.

## Week by week

### Week 1: 19 October to 25 October

**Goal:** Statistics and SQL, the two skills nearly every data screen tests first.

**Modules:** [Probability and statistics](#/module/probability-statistics?d=plain) (137 cards) and [SQL and data wrangling](#/module/sql-analytics?d=plain) (118 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Write three SQL queries a day from the [Questions page](#/questions), filtered to module SQL and data wrangling (37 questions), and say the grain of each table before you join. Solve two probability puzzles a day, then check each one in code.

**Videos (optional):** CS229 Lectures 2 and 3 (linear regression, maximum likelihood, logistic regression).

**You are ready to move on when:** You can explain a p value and a confidence interval in two sentences each, and you write a window function query without looking anything up.

**Sunday mock:** three SQL questions in 45 minutes, then five statistics questions aloud.

### Week 2: 26 October to 1 November

**Goal:** The classical ML toolkit and the from scratch coding round.

**Modules:** [Classical machine learning](#/module/classical-ml?d=plain) (150 cards) and [ML coding](#/module/ml-coding?d=plain) (110 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Implement one model a day from an empty file (linear regression, logistic regression, k-means, a decision tree split, ROC AUC) and test it against scikit-learn on random data. The [ML coding guide](#/module/ml-coding?d=deep) has working versions.

**Videos (optional):** CS229 Lectures 6 and 9 (bias and variance, then k-means and Gaussian mixtures).

**You are ready to move on when:** You can explain the bias and variance trade off, regularization and leakage with an example each, and you finish k-means in 20 minutes.

**Sunday mock:** ten classical ML questions aloud from the Questions page (module Classical machine learning, 68 questions), then implement logistic regression in 25 minutes.

### Week 3: 2 November to 8 November

**Goal:** Neural networks and the transformer, the base for every LLM question.

**Modules:** [Deep learning foundations](#/module/deep-learning?d=plain) (118 cards) and [Transformers and LLMs](#/module/transformers-llms?d=plain) (124 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Write attention with a causal mask in NumPy, then derive backpropagation for a two layer network on paper. Compare your attention output with a small PyTorch version.

**Videos (optional):** Zero to Hero Video 1 (micrograd) and CME295 Lecture 1 (the transformer).

**You are ready to move on when:** You can explain self attention, the KV cache and why we divide by the square root of the key size, each in 30 seconds.

**Sunday mock:** explain a transformer block aloud for two minutes, then answer five questions from the Questions page (module Transformers and LLMs, 63 questions).

### Week 4: 9 November to 15 November

**Goal:** How models are tuned and made to reason, plus how to run an A/B test.

**Modules:** [Post training and reasoning](#/module/post-training-reasoning?d=plain) (134 cards) and [Experiments and causal inference](#/module/experimentation-causal?d=plain) (113 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Explain RLHF, DPO and GRPO in plain words, then sketch an A/B test: metric, sample size, duration and three pitfalls. Use the experiments cards to check your sample size arithmetic.

**Videos (optional):** CME295 Lectures 5 and 6 (preference tuning, reasoning models).

**You are ready to move on when:** You can say how LoRA, RLHF, DPO and GRPO differ, and you can design a test and size it by hand.

**Sunday mock:** a 30 minute experiment design case, for example comparing two versions of a user flow.

### Week 5: 16 November to 22 November

**Goal:** LLM applications and the system design round.

**Modules:** [RAG, agents and evals](#/module/rag-agents-evals?d=plain) (150 cards) and [ML system design](#/module/ml-system-design?d=plain) (115 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Do two timed designs aloud: a RAG assistant for company documents and a feed or ads ranking system. Use the eleven step framework in the [ML system design guide](#/module/ml-system-design?d=deep) and say a number at every step.

**Videos (optional):** CME295 Lectures 7 and 8 (RAG, tools and agents, then evaluation).

**You are ready to move on when:** You can answer "how would you know your RAG system works?" and finish a 45 minute design with a rollout and monitoring plan.

**Sunday mock:** a 45 minute system design round with a friend interrupting, using a prompt from the Questions page (module ML system design, 47 questions).

### Week 6: 23 November to 29 November

**Goal:** Product cases and your own story, then a full rehearsal.

**Modules:** [Product analytics and cases](#/module/product-analytics?d=plain) (113 cards) and [Narration and your projects](#/module/narration?d=plain) (119 cards). Tick only these two on the Learn page. Spend about two thirds of your reading and practice time on the first and the rest on the second.

**Practice:** Work one metric drop and one metric definition case a day aloud using the five moves in the [product analytics guide](#/module/product-analytics?d=deep). Write six behavioral stories and your project talk at three lengths.

**Videos (optional):** None. Use the time to repeat weak cards and rehearse.

**You are ready to move on when:** You have a one minute self introduction, a story bank and a five minute talk for each project.

**Sunday mock:** a full loop rehearsal, with one behavioral round, one project deep dive and one technical round for your target role.

## The two week crash version

Use this when an interview is two weeks away. It covers eight modules. Six are the ones that hold the most questions in the bank: Classical machine learning has 68, Transformers and LLMs 63, ML system design 47, Probability and statistics 45, ML coding 38 and SQL 37. The other two are Narration, because every loop starts with your story, and RAG, agents and evals, because AI engineer loops lean on it. If you target analyst roles, swap ML coding, Transformers and RAG for Product analytics, Experiments and a second SQL day, and read the [interview loops guide](#/guide/question-bank/interview-loops) first to see which rounds your role gets.

The eight modules hold 1,023 cards, far more than two weeks allow. So the crash version trades completeness for coverage:

1. **Read the cheatsheet first.** Each cheatsheet is a one page dense reference. Read it aloud once at the start of the module's days.
2. **Use the module deck with a clock.** Open the module's deck from the Learn page. It serves due cards, then new cards in order, so each day continues where yesterday stopped. Stop when the time block ends and do not try to finish the module.
3. **Drill daily.** Five questions a day from the module you are on, 45 seconds each.
4. **Mock early.** Two mocks in the second week tell you more than another day of cards.

| Day | Focus | Block plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Narration and your projects](#/module/narration?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, then write a one minute introduction, a story bank of six stories and a five minute talk for each project. Every loop starts here. |
| 2 | [SQL and data wrangling](#/module/sql-analytics?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of the module deck, then five SQL questions from the Questions page against a clock. |
| 3 | SQL, then Python and pandas | 30 minutes of cards, then ten small pandas tasks ending in a sanity check. |
| 4 | [Probability and statistics](#/module/probability-statistics?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of cards, then five probability puzzles solved on paper and checked in code. |
| 5 | [Classical machine learning](#/module/classical-ml?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of cards, then ten ML concept questions aloud. |
| 6 | Classical ML, then [ML coding](#/module/ml-coding?d=cheat) | 30 minutes of cards, then implement k-means and logistic regression from an empty file. |
| 7 | Rest | Reviews only, 20 minutes, then the Weakest twenty deck. |
| 8 | [Transformers and LLMs](#/module/transformers-llms?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of cards, then explain attention aloud for two minutes. |
| 9 | [RAG, agents and evals](#/module/rag-agents-evals?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of cards, then answer "how would you evaluate a RAG system?" aloud. |
| 10 | [ML system design](#/module/ml-system-design?d=cheat) | Cheatsheet, 60 minutes of cards, then one timed 45 minute design aloud. |
| 11 | ML system design again | A second design on a different prompt, plus the Weakest twenty deck. |
| 12 | Mock one | A timed mock of the round your role is most likely to open with, from the loops guide. |
| 13 | Mock two | A second mock of a different round, plus a story rehearsal. |
| 14 | Light review | Reread the cheatsheets, run the Weakest twenty deck, sleep. |

If you have a coding screen, move two crash days to ML coding. If your loop has an AI assisted round, practice it once with the assistant you will be given, as described in the loops guide.

## Keeping the plan honest

- Open the [Progress page](#/progress) every Sunday. It shows how many cards you have learned, and you can export your progress from there.
- Count only cards you can answer aloud. If your learned count is ahead of your drill results, slow down.
- Add every question you miss in a mock to your next day's Drill list.
- Treat the plan as a ceiling. A week where you finish one module well is better than two modules half learned.
